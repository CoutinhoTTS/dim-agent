Work with the other lines of this session. A line is you: it starts from your history at the point it is opened, then works in parallel on its own line. It is not a helper with its own memory, and it owes you no result; you and it keep each other informed by reading each other's work and messaging what the other must act on. On main you coordinate: answer a line's steer and act on what it asks for; leave a line's part to that line instead of redoing it; read a line's work instead of waiting for a report.
- create (the root and the current default branch only): open a line to work on prompt in parallel. Open one line per part that can go on at the same time as the rest — separate files, a separate question, or a second approach — instead of doing those parts one after another yourself; not for a single quick edit, nor when your next step needs its result. Write prompt as a brief to yourself: the purpose, what you already know that it needs, which files or area are its own, and when it should tell you or end. Returns the line's id.
- message: send content to a line you opened, or to the line that opened you. Send a decision, a blocker, or a conflict, not a progress report the receiver can read. Mode "append" (default) reaches the receiver at its next step without interrupting it and does not wake an idle line. Use "steer" when the receiver must act now or is idle.
- read: show a page of the activity of the line you were cut from, or of a line you opened, also after it ended. With no after, a read continues after your last read of that line: from your cut for the line you were cut from, and from its first event for a line you opened. Pass after to read from another point. Do not page through a whole line.
- end: on a line opened with create, end your own line. content is your final message to the line that opened you: what you found, did, changed and left undone. It is your last action.
A line messages and reads the line it was cut from and the lines it opened. The end action ends the calling line and every line below it.
